Look What’s New: A Beginner’s Guide to Badminton

Welcome to Playfield, an information page about active pastimes people enjoy in parks, halls and backyards. Today’s topic is badminton, a racket sport where players send a feathered shuttle back and forth over a net.

This guide explains the court, the equipment, the formats, the scoring, the main shots and some warm-up tips, with a picture for each topic.

The court

There’s More to Explore

Badminton is played on a rectangular court split in half by a net. A standard court is 13.4 metres long and 6.1 metres wide for doubles. For singles, the court is a little narrower, about 5.18 metres wide.

The net stands about 1.55 metres high at the edges, so it is taller than most people expect. Painted lines show where the shuttle must land and where the serve must go.

Equipment

Find What You Need

Playfield lists only the basics here, because badminton needs very little to get started.

  • Racket: light, usually about 85 grams, with a strung oval head and a long shaft. A lighter racket is easier for beginners to swing.
  • Shuttlecock: a small cork base with 16 feathers (or a nylon skirt). The shape makes it slow down quickly and fly in a stable way.
  • Net and posts: set up across the middle of the court.
  • Shoes: light indoor shoes with good grip for quick side-to-side steps.
  • Clothing: comfortable, breathable clothes that let your arms and legs move freely.
Formats

See What’s Available

Singles

One player on each side. The court is narrower but longer in use, so stamina matters most.

Doubles

Two players per side on the full-width court. Teamwork and quick reactions are the key.

Mixed doubles

Each team has one woman and one man. It rewards good communication between partners.

Rules

Discover Quality Choices

The rules are short and easy to follow.

  1. Serve: hit the shuttle underarm, below waist height, diagonally across the net into the opposite service box.
  2. Rally: after the serve, players hit the shuttle back and forth. It must not touch the ground on your side.
  3. Points: every rally gives a point to someone, no matter who served.
  4. Winning a set: the first side to 21 points wins, but must lead by at least 2 points. If the score reaches 29–29, the next point wins at 30.
  5. Match: most matches are the best of three sets.
  6. Faults: hitting the net, landing outside the lines, or hitting the shuttle twice in a row all give a point to the other side.
Shots

Make Your Next Move

Four basic shots cover most of what you will use. Practise each one slowly, then add speed.

Clear

A high, deep shot that pushes your opponent to the back line and gives you time.

Smash

A powerful downward hit. Use it when the shuttle is high above the net.

Drop

A soft, short shot that falls just over the net and pulls your opponent forward.

Drive

A fast, flat shot across the court at about shoulder height, often used in doubles.

Wrap-up

Start Exploring Today

Badminton is friendly to beginners and keeps getting more interesting as you improve. Learn the court, hold the racket loosely, practise the four shots and warm up before each session. Playfield hopes this guide helps you understand the sport and enjoy it safely with family and friends.

Fitness

Constant movement works legs, arms and breathing.

Reflexes

Fast exchanges train quick reactions.

Teamwork

Doubles builds trust and clear communication.

Low cost

A racket, a shuttle and some open space are enough.

Warm-up and safety

Spend five to ten minutes on gentle jogging, arm circles and leg stretches before you play. Drink water often, wear grippy shoes and rest if you feel pain. Outdoors, play where wind is light, because wind moves the shuttle.

Common questions

Can I play outside?

Yes, on a calm day. Wind changes the shuttle’s path, so indoor halls are better for serious practice.

What if the shuttle lands on the line?

It counts as in. The lines are part of the court.

Do I need lessons?

Not to start. Many people learn the basics by playing, and a coach helps later with technique.
